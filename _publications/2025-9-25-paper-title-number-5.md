Using the dinuclear system model, this study examined the reaction systems employing <sup>242</sup>Cm to <sup>248</sup>Cm targets with <sup>45</sup>Sc, <sup>50</sup>Ti, <sup>51</sup>V, <sup>54</sup>Cr, and <sup>55</sup>Mn projectiles, providing possible reaction systems toward elements 119–121. The predicted maximum evaporation-residue cross sections generally increase as the curium target becomes more neutron rich, while also showing a clear odd-even staggering pattern. By separating the capture, fusion, and survival stages, the study shows that the isotope dependence arises mainly from the survival process. <sup>247</sup>Cm consequently emerges as a promising candidate target for future experiments.
